# Course Resources & Reading Map

Curated books, free courses, companion repositories, and canonical papers for **Lifecycle of Artificial Intelligence Systems**, mapped to each week of `docs/syllabus.md`.

> How to use this. Each week's lecture/lab is self-contained; these resources are for students who want depth or an alternative explanation. The per-week reading map at the bottom points to the *most relevant* chapter(s) for that week — read those, not whole books. Chapter **titles** are stable across printings; **page numbers vary by edition**, so confirm against your copy. All tool docs are the official quickstarts already cited in each lab README.

## 1. Recommended books

### Anchor texts (used most often)

- **Chip Huyen — _Designing Machine Learning Systems_** (O'Reilly, 2022). The course's conceptual spine: lifecycle thinking, data engineering, deployment, distribution shifts, monitoring, infrastructure. Referenced in most weeks. https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/designing-machine-learning/9781098107956/
- **Noah Gift & Alfredo Deza — _Practical MLOps_** (O'Reilly, 2021). Hands-on companion: containers, CI/CD, serving, monitoring/logging. Closest in spirit to the labs. https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/practical-mlops/9781098103002/

### Additional recommended books

- **Mark Treveil & the Dataiku Team — _Introducing MLOps_** (O'Reilly, 2020). Short, role- and process-oriented overview (lifecycle, governance, team responsibilities). Best for Weeks 1 and 13. https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/introducing-mlops/9781492083283/
- **V. Lakshmanan, S. Robinson, M. Munn — _Machine Learning Design Patterns_** (O'Reilly, 2020). 30 named patterns; especially the **Reproducibility** patterns (Transform, Repeatable Splitting, Workflow Pipeline, Bridged Schema) and Responsible-AI patterns. Best for Weeks 2–6, 10. https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/machine-learning-design/9781098115777/
- **Cathy Chen, Niall Murphy, Kranti Parisa, D. Sculley, Todd Underwood — _Reliable Machine Learning_** (O'Reilly, 2022). Applies Google SRE practice to ML: SLIs/SLOs, monitoring, incident response, postmortems. Best for Weeks 11–13. https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/reliable-machine-learning/9781098106218/
- **Chip Huyen — _AI Engineering: Building Applications with Foundation Models_** (O'Reilly, 2025). For the Week 14 LLMOps outlook: the foundation-model stack, evaluation, RAG, agents, inference optimization. https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/ai-engineering/9781098166298/

> Access note: all six are on the O'Reilly learning platform (most university libraries provide access). Where a student has no O'Reilly access, the free courses and papers below cover the same ground.

## 2. Free courses & companion repositories

- **MLOps Zoomcamp — DataTalks.Club** (free, open-source). The single closest companion to this course: its stack is **MLflow, Prefect, Docker, Prometheus, Grafana, GitHub Actions, Evidently** — almost our exact toolset. Use module-by-module as an alternative walkthrough. https://github.com/DataTalksClub/mlops-zoomcamp
- **Made With ML — Goku Mohandas** (free). Strong on software-engineering rigor: testing, pre-commit, CI/CD with GitHub Actions, experiment tracking, serving. Best for Weeks 3, 6, 7, 9. https://github.com/GokuMohandas/Made-With-ML · course: https://madewithml.com/
- **MLOps-Basics — Ravi Raja (`graviraja`)** (free). A week-by-week build of one project across DVC, MLflow/W&B, Docker, FastAPI, ONNX, and monitoring — useful as a structural mirror of our running-example approach. https://github.com/graviraja/MLOps-Basics
- **ml-ops.org — INNOQ** (free, CC-BY). Concise reference on MLOps principles and the end-to-end ML workflow; good first reading for Week 1. https://ml-ops.org/
- **Full Stack Deep Learning** (free lectures). Project-level view of building and shipping ML products. Supplemental for Weeks 1, 7, 9. https://fullstackdeeplearning.com/

## 3. Canonical papers & industry guides

- **D. Sculley et al. — "Hidden Technical Debt in Machine Learning Systems"** (NeurIPS 2015). The founding argument for MLOps (CACE, glue code, pipeline jungles, undeclared consumers). Week 1. https://papers.nips.cc/paper/5656-hidden-technical-debt-in-machine-learning-systems
- **Martin Zinkevich — "Rules of Machine Learning: Best Practices for ML Engineering"** (Google). 43 practical rules incl. training/serving-skew defenses. Weeks 1, 5, 6. https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/guides/rules-of-ml
- **Google Cloud — "MLOps: Continuous delivery and automation pipelines in machine learning"** (whitepaper). The MLOps maturity levels 0/1/2 and CI/CD/CT framing. Weeks 1, 7, 8. https://cloud.google.com/architecture/mlops-continuous-delivery-and-automation-pipelines-in-machine-learning
- **Google Cloud — "Practitioners Guide to MLOps"** (whitepaper). The MLOps capabilities/process model. Weeks 1, 10. https://cloud.google.com/resources/mlops-whitepaper
- **E. Breck et al. — "The ML Test Score: A Rubric for ML Production Readiness and Technical Debt Reduction"** (Google, 2017). Concrete data/model/infra/monitoring tests. Weeks 5, 6, 7. https://research.google/pubs/the-ml-test-score-a-rubric-for-ml-production-readiness-and-technical-debt-reduction/
- **N. Polyzotis et al. — "Data Validation for Machine Learning"** (SysML 2019). Schema-based data validation and training-serving skew. Week 5. https://mlsys.org/Conferences/2019/doc/2019/167.pdf
- **D. Sculley et al. — "Hidden Technical Debt in Machine Learning Systems"** (NeurIPS 2015). Why ML CI is not ordinary CI: the CACE principle ("changing anything changes everything"), pipeline jungles, and configuration debt. Weeks 7-8. https://papers.nips.cc/paper/5656-hidden-technical-debt-in-machine-learning-systems.pdf
- **J. Buolamwini & T. Gebru — "Gender Shades: Intersectional Accuracy Disparities in Commercial Gender Classification"** (PMLR v81, FAT* 2018). The canonical demonstration that an aggregate metric hides a group: error rates from 0.8% to 34.7% inside products reporting strong overall accuracy. Week 6. https://proceedings.mlr.press/v81/buolamwini18a.html
- **Z. Obermeyer et al. — "Dissecting racial bias in an algorithm used to manage the health of populations"** (*Science*, 2019). Cost used as a proxy for need; the model was accurate and the objective was wrong. The failure no evaluation gate can catch. Week 6. https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aax2342
- **A. Chouldechova (2017)** and **J. Kleinberg, S. Mullainathan & M. Raghavan (2016)**. Why calibration and error-rate balance cannot both hold except in degenerate cases — so choosing a fairness criterion is an engineering decision, not a configuration one. Week 6. https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.00056 · https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.05807
- **M. Mitchell et al. — "Model Cards for Model Reporting"** (FAT* 2019). The Model Card template used in Week 13. https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.03993
- **T. Gebru et al. — "Datasheets for Datasets"** (2018/2021). Dataset documentation companion to Model Cards. Weeks 5, 13. https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09010
- **J. Gama et al. — "A Survey on Concept Drift Adaptation"** (ACM Computing Surveys, 2014). The reference taxonomy of drift. Week 12. https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2523813
- **P. Lewis et al. — "Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks"** (NeurIPS 2020). Where retrieval-augmented generation starts. Week 14. https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401
- **L. Zheng et al. — "Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena"** (2023). Establishes the LLM-as-a-judge method *and* names its systematic failure modes (position, verbosity, self-enhancement). Read both halves — the 80%-agreement figure is quoted far more often than the biases. Week 14. https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685
